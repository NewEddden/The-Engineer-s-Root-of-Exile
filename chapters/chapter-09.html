Chapter 9 - The Summons

The old floorboards creaked under Ward's steps, fast enough that Emeric knew before the door opened.

"We might have a problem." Ward held out a folded letter, still sealed. "Came direct. I've seen this stock before. It's the royal court's summons paper."

Emeric took it and turned it over once. Randillia crest, pressed deep, no wax cracked from handling. This had come by rider and not by relay, which meant somebody had paid for it to arrive in one pair of hands.

"Well," he said. "The baron's decided to litigate."

He heard the word go out of his mouth and land nowhere. There wasn't a court in this world that used it. There was a word here that meant almost the same thing, and he had it, and he'd reached past it out of pure reflex for one that belonged to a dead man in a diner three months and one lifetime ago.

Ward didn't ask. He had stopped asking about most of what came out of the prince's mouth these days, which Emeric had at some point decided to find comforting rather than lonely and had not entirely managed.

He broke the seal and read fast. Structure first, content second.

By order of the crown's judiciary, on petition of Baron Wexler of Kaldo, Prince Emeric Stelan Randillia is called to answer questions regarding the administration of Kaldo barony, specifically financial irregularities alleging misuse of discharged debt records. Advocate Keller has been assigned to receive testimony and evidence from all relevant parties before a formal hearing.

He read it again. It didn't change.

"He petitioned first," Emeric said.

Ward's jaw moved, once. "Meaning?"

"Meaning I know exactly how this works. I don't get to run it. He does. As of six days ago I'm not the man bringing evidence to Keller. I'm the man Keller has been told to look at."

He set the letter flat on the desk and smoothed a crease that wasn't there, and then stood looking at it, because the second thing had just arrived on top of the first and it was worse.

You wrote it carefully. I noticed.

Three months ago he had sat in this room and drafted a letter three times until it stopped sounding like a request and started sounding like a procedure that had always existed, because a baron can sit on a request and cannot sit on an instruction under seal without a steward having to explain the delay to somebody.

He had been rather pleased with it. He had walked into Wexler's office and the man had turned it over in his hands and told him, plainly, to his face, that he had noticed.

And then he had gone away and used it back. Same move. Same reasoning. Make it procedure, make it first, make the other man the one who has to explain himself.

"I taught him this," Emeric said. "In one afternoon. He watched me do it once and he did it better."

"You couldn't have known."

"I could have thought about it for four seconds. That's all it needed." He picked the letter up again. "Every technique I use in front of a man is a technique I've given him. I know that. I've known it since I was old enough to read a room. And I handed this one over gift-wrapped because I wanted to be clever at somebody."

Ward read the letter twice himself before handing it back. "How long do we have?"

"Petition's dated six days ago. Travel, plus processing." Emeric checked the back for a return date he already knew wasn't there. "So whatever Wexler told them, they've been sitting with it the better part of a week. We're not early. We're catching up."

"You said Keller. You know him."

"I know of him. I sat through two of his hearings when I was seventeen, out of boredom, and I remember them better than I have any right to." He almost laughed. "Turns out being drunk at court for a decade is a reasonable education in how a court works, so long as you don't mind who you had to be to get it."

"And?"

"He's fair. That's the problem. He won't take my word for anything on the strength of my name, and he shouldn't. He'll want what Ferra has, and Ferra has a route and not a name."

"Is that enough to walk in with?"

"It's enough to walk in with something instead of nothing. It isn't enough to walk in and win."

Ward set the practice blade he'd carried up out of habit against the doorframe. "You're going yourself."

It wasn't a question. Emeric answered it like one anyway.

"I'm not sending a courier to answer a summons with my name on it. That's not how this works, and even if it were, I wouldn't do it that way."

"That's not an engineering answer."

"No." Emeric almost smiled. "It isn't."

There was one thing he had to settle before the horses.

"Petram rides with us. Declan and Osric on him the whole way, and the moment we reach the capital he goes into the judiciary's custody, not mine and not anybody's in Kaldo."

Ward considered it. "He's your only witness."

"He's my only witness and I'm about to be four days away from the room he's sleeping in, in a castle where somebody found a nineteen-year-old inside four hours." Emeric shook his head. "If I leave him here he's dead inside a week and it'll look like a fever. If I hand him to the crown, then whoever wants him quiet has to reach into a building that hangs people for that."

"And if they get to him anyway?"

"Then I'll know something about how far up this goes, which is more than I know tonight."

He found Ferra before the hour was out, the letter folded in his coat where he could feel the edge of it against his ribs like a splinter he hadn't pulled. She read it once, ledger-speed, the way she read everything.

"He isn't accusing you of anything specific. Look at the wording. Irregularities. Financial administration. He hasn't named Cobb, hasn't named the debt, hasn't named a figure."

"Because he doesn't know what I have."

"He doesn't know what you have," Ferra agreed. "But he knows something's moving. That's why he went first. Not because he's confident. Because he isn't."

Emeric turned that over the way he turned the stone over, without quite meaning to. A man sure of his footing doesn't need to define the ground before anybody else gets a vote on it. Whatever the petition technically was, it wasn't a man closing a door. It was a man choosing which room the fight happened in, which was, he thought, exactly what you do when you have no idea how big the fight is.

"What do you need to get the route findings to something Keller can't wave off as speculation?"

Ferra didn't answer straight away, which from her was its own answer. "More time than you're going to give me. But I can get you closer. Not proof. Closer."

"Closer is what I've got room for."

She almost smiled at that, tired around the edges of it. "Everything's what you've got room for, lately."

He left before dark. Ward didn't argue about coming, which meant he had already decided he was, and Emeric didn't waste breath pretending otherwise. Ferra's half-finished work would follow on the fastest rider Bram trusted with something this quiet.

The last thing he did before the horses were saddled was find the stone in his pocket and turn it once between two fingers, the reflex it had become without asking him.

Whatever he'd found underneath the loop three nights ago was still down there. Unnamed. Unfiled. Waiting the way it had waited before he'd ever noticed it, on the grounds that waiting appeared to be the entire thing it did.

He didn't have time to go back and look at it properly. So he filed it too, next to a boy's name and a date in a week he hadn't been in the country for. One more thing that would have to wait its turn to be understood.

He rode for the capital not knowing which of the things he was carrying would matter most before this was finished, which he had learned by now was the normal condition of carrying anything at all.
