Chapter 9 - The Summons

The old floorboards creaked under Ward's steps, fast enough that Emeric knew before the door opened.

"We might have a problem." Ward held out a folded letter, still sealed. "Came direct from the capital. I've seen this packaging before. Royal court summons stock."

Emeric turned it over once. Randillia crest, pressed deep, no cracks in the wax from handling. This had come by rider, not relay, which meant somebody had decided it shouldn't sit in a bag with anything else.

He broke it and read the way he read everything now. Structure first.

By order of the crown's judiciary, on petition of Baron Wexler of Kaldo, Prince Emeric Stelan Randillia was called to answer questions regarding the administration of Kaldo barony, specifically financial irregularities alleging misuse of discharged debt records. Advocate Keller had been assigned to receive testimony and evidence from all relevant parties in advance of a formal hearing.

He read it again. It didn't change.

"He petitioned first," Emeric said. "Before I had anything to bring Keller myself."

Ward's jaw moved, once. "Meaning?"

"Meaning I know how this process works and I don't get to run it. He does." Emeric set the letter flat on the desk and smoothed a crease that wasn't there. "Today I'm not the man bringing evidence to Keller. I'm the one Keller has been told to look into."

"You've had judiciary business before." Not quite a question. Ward's voice had the particular flatness it got when he already knew and was letting Emeric say it.

"Once." He didn't dress it up. Some of the memory was his and some of it was the other man's, and this one was entirely Emeric's, which made it worse. "A gambling house wanted a great deal of money from me that I didn't have. My father's people made it go away through that office and I never asked how. I was nineteen. I remember being annoyed at how long it took."

Ward said nothing, which was its own commentary.

"How bad is it," he said eventually. "If it goes against you."

That was the question Emeric had been avoiding since the second read, so he made himself answer it out loud.

"They can't do much to me personally. I'm already in the last place the family sends people." He tapped the page. "What they can do is find that a resident prince has been rummaging through a baron's accounts out of grievance. That's not a punishment. That's a label. It goes on the record and it stays there, and every finding I ever bring out of this barony afterward gets read through it. Bad water, bad ledgers, bad anything. The prince with a grudge said so."

"So he doesn't need to win," Ward said slowly. "He needs it to be about you."

"That's the whole petition. That's why he wrote it before I could."

Ward set the practice blade he'd carried up out of habit against the doorframe.

"Take Petram."

"Thought about it on the first read." Emeric shook his head. "A bound man gives a court exactly what a bound man is worth, which is nothing, and the moment his name is spoken in a judiciary chamber he stops being useful and starts being loose. He heard what happened to the last person who overheard him say Cobb out loud." He put the letter in his coat. "He stays in the cell and he stays alive, and nobody outside this castle learns he can talk."

"Then what do you walk in with?"

"Ferra."

He found her before the hour was out, the letter riding against his ribs like a splinter he hadn't pulled. She read it once, faster than Ward had, ledger-speed.

"He's not accusing you of anything specific. Look at the wording. Irregularities. Financial administration. He hasn't named Cobb, hasn't named the debt, hasn't named a figure."

"Because he doesn't know what I have."

"He doesn't know what you have," Ferra agreed. "But he knows something is moving. That's why he went first. Not because he's confident. Because he isn't."

Emeric turned that over the way he turned the crystal over, without quite meaning to. A man sure of his footing doesn't need to define the ground before anyone else gets a vote on it. Whatever the petition technically was, it wasn't a man closing a door. It was a man choosing which room the fight happened in.

"What do you need," he asked, "to get the route findings to something Keller can't wave off as speculation?"

Ferra didn't answer right away, which from her was its own kind of answer.

"I want to be clear with you, because you're going to hear what you want to hear otherwise." She squared the pages in front of her. "I can prove those entries were altered. I can prove what was underneath. I cannot prove who scraped them, and I cannot put a coin from that treasury into Cobb's hand. Not from paper I have. Not in the time you've got."

"How close does it get?"

"Close enough that an honest man reading it has to explain it to himself." She looked up. "That's the whole of what I'm selling you. If your advocate isn't honest, it's worth nothing at all."

"He's honest. That's the part that scares me."

"Then you'd better hope the baron says something in that room he can't take back," Ferra said, "because I can't hand you a hanging."

She said it plainly and without any weight on it, and went back to the pages, and Emeric found that he was less unsettled by it than he should have been. Somebody telling him precisely where the edge of their competence sat was becoming the rarest thing he encountered in this world.

He left before dark. Ward didn't argue about coming, which meant he had already decided, and Emeric didn't waste breath pretending otherwise. Ferra would follow with whatever she had finished, on the fastest rider Bram trusted with something this quiet.

The last thing he did before the horses were saddled was find the crystal in his pocket and turn it once between two fingers, the reflex it had become without his permission.

Whatever he had found underneath the loop was still there. Unnamed. Unfiled. Waiting the way it had waited before he'd ever noticed it, and costing him a night every time he went down to look. He didn't have a night to spend.

So he filed that too, next to Tam's name and the discharged debt's date and the third possibility he'd written down and had nothing to support, and rode for the capital carrying more unresolved things than he'd have liked to admit to anyone who asked.
