Chapter 14 - Kaldo Iron

Bram didn't so much fall as arrive somewhere else, the transition leaving out the part where a person notices he's moving at all. One moment his hand was closing on a shoulder that had gone, all at once, less solid than a shoulder should be. The next he was standing in a room he half knew, with Emeric across from him looking every bit as thrown as he felt.

"What," Bram said, and got no further.

It was the office, or near enough that Bram's first instinct was to check the window for the view it always had. The view held, mostly. The details went soft at the edges the way a memory does and a real room doesn't, sharp where they needed to be and vague everywhere else.

Off at the corner of his sight, where nothing had any business floating, hung lines of text and shapes that meant nothing to him and plainly everything to whatever part of Emeric's mind had put this place together.

"You're not supposed to be here," Emeric said, which managed to land worse than saying nothing.

"I'm not supposed to be anywhere but my own kitchen," Bram said. "Where's here, exactly?"

Emeric didn't have a clean answer. He gave what he could without it sounding like madness. The delving space. The Control Rune. The fact that he had wanted Bram to see the furnace badly enough that the wanting had, somehow, done the work the stone usually did.

He left out the part he had only just arrived at himself, which was that he had reached into another man and pulled him somewhere without asking and without any idea how he'd managed it, and could not at present undo it or repeat it on purpose or promise it wouldn't happen again the next time he wanted something badly enough.

He watched Bram's face while he talked. Watched it go blank while the man decided whether to be afraid, then watched him land on something nearer stubborn curiosity instead.

"You said you can build things in here," Bram said, slow. "Things that don't exist yet."

"I think so. I've never tried before now."

"Try."

Emeric turned toward the open space in the middle of the room and thought about the furnace the way he had thought about it a hundred times already. The charcoal bed. The fire crystal seated where it would pull the most even heat. The air crystal's line running straight to the melt instead of the firebox.

It came together slower than he had expected, the pieces resolving out of nothing the way a sketch fills in under a pencil, except here the pencil was only attention and the paper was whatever this place actually was underneath the office it was pretending to be.

Bram walked around the finished shape twice before he said a word, running a hand down a wall of it that wasn't there, his fingers passing through with no resistance and, somehow, no doubt either.

"The air line." He tapped the space where it entered the melt chamber. "This part. You've got it coming in at a straight angle, fast as you can push it."

"That's the design. More air through the melt, faster the impurities burn off."

"Won't work like that. Not with our iron." Bram crouched, reading the angle the way only a man who had stood in front of a lot of metal could read it, seeing a thing there that no amount of explaining could put into another man's head. "Kaldo ore's got more sulfur running through it than whatever you learned on. Blast it straight and fast like that and you'll cook the melt uneven. Pockets that never purify right, sitting next to spots that burn hot enough to ruin the temper. I've scrapped good iron for less than what you're drawing."

Emeric looked at the design again. Really looked this time, instead of trusting a solution that had felt clean because it had felt familiar.

Bram was right, and he could see exactly why the second it was said, the same way a valve sized for one system fails the moment you drop it into another running at a slightly different pressure. His information hadn't been wrong. It had been built for iron that wasn't this iron.

"Angle it lower," Bram said, already reaching for the shape as though it were something his hands could work, and in here, it turned out, they could. "Slower push. Wider spread across the surface instead of straight down the middle. Takes longer. Comes out even."

They worked it through together after that, Emeric holding the parts that wanted logic and order, Bram fixing the parts that wanted thirty years of standing at a forge and learning what metal does to you when you are wrong about it.

It didn't feel like teaching, from either side of it. It felt like two people who each knew half of something finally getting to work the same half at the same time.

Neither of them kept track of how long it ran. The light in the false office never shifted, which ought to have unsettled them both and somehow didn't, not once they were too far into the work to look up.

When they finally stepped back, the furnace looked right in a way the drawing never had. Bram stood with his arms crossed, and what he was studying it with had moved off wariness and onto something a good deal closer to pride.

"I still don't know what to call this place," he said.

"Neither do I," Emeric admitted. "I didn't know it could do this until about an hour ago, best I can tell."

"An hour." Bram looked at the window that wasn't a window. "Doesn't feel like an hour."

"It might not have been. Time doesn't sit the same in here, from the little I understand of it." He didn't add the rest, which was that whatever the hours in here cost, they came out of the same account as sleep, and he had no idea at all whether the bill for this one was going to arrive on Bram's side of the table or his.

Bram didn't look glad to hear any of it. He didn't look like a man about to bolt either. "Ferra's going to have questions."

"Ferra's going to have questions," Emeric agreed, already dreading it a little, knowing precisely how sharp those questions would run and how few good answers he had on hand. "Let's go find out how many."

He thought about surfacing the same loose way he had thought about the furnace, and the office came back the way water finds its own level. No jolt to it, only a return to weight and light and the flat ache of having sat too still for too long. Bram came back a half second behind him, the two of them blinking in a room that hadn't moved an inch while they were elsewhere.

Ferra was standing over them, both hands braced on the table, a ledger knocked half off its stack and left where it had fallen on the floor.
